Get the Facts

The Basics

Q: What exactly is this amendment?

A: Amendment 1 creates a non-partisan redistricting commission. Instead of politicians picking their voters, this amendment empowers a balanced group of citizens and legislators to draw fair district lines based on data, not politics.

Q: Who draws the lines currently?

A: Currently, legislators draw their own districts. This conflict of interest has led to gerrymandered maps that protect incumbents. Amendment 1 ends this practice by requiring bipartisan consensus and citizen input.

Q: Is this a permanent change?

A: No. This is a specific constitutional fix for the 2026 redistricting cycle. Any future permanent changes would require a separate vote, but this ensures fairness for the next decade of elections.

Q: Is the April 21 special election still happening?

A: Yes. Despite ongoing legal appeals in the Virginia Supreme Court, the election remains scheduled for April 21. We are moving forward to ensure Virginians have their say.

Impact on You

Q: Will my polling place change?

A: Likely not. Redistricting changes who represents you in Richmond and Washington, but local precincts are managed by your city or county. If your polling place does change, your local registrar will notify you by mail.

Q: How does this help minority representation?

A: It strengthens it. The amendment explicitly requires compliance with the Voting Rights Act and adds state-level protections to prevent the dilution of minority voting power. Civil rights organizations like the NAACP have endorsed this amendment for that reason.

What's At Stake

Q: What happens if this fails?

A: We go back to court. If Amendment 1 is rejected, the drawing of maps will likely be decided by the Virginia Supreme Court, a process that is less transparent and provides zero opportunity for public input. Voting YES keeps the power with the people.
